Fatigue & wellness

Tired of feeling tired?

Fatigue can be a sign of underlying health issues, affecting energy levels and overall wellness. Koala describes the pattern many patients come to accept as normal: never feeling rested, not being able to sleep through the night without waking several times, needing a daily nap just to get through the day. Fatigue also brings irritability, lack of concentration, and an inability to do the things you would like to do.

Koala's page cites over 18 million people in the United States with obstructive sleep apnea, and an estimate that over 90 percent go undiagnosed. Untreated sleep apnea can also increase the risk for high blood pressure, heart disease, depression, headaches, weight gain, stroke, and drowsy driving.

For obstructive sleep apnea, Koala offers oral appliance therapy: a device that holds the jaw in a position that allows the airway to remain as open and firm as possible during sleep. Oral appliances are similar to athletic mouth guards but less bulky and completely non-invasive. Read more under Oral Appliances.

Sleep disorders

Conditions that keep the body from resting.

Sleep disorders, such as insomnia and sleep apnea, disrupt the body's ability to rest and recover. These conditions can lead to fatigue, decreased concentration, and long-term health issues if left untreated.

Koala provides comprehensive evaluations and treatments for sleep-related conditions like sleep apnea, snoring, insomnia, and parasomnias, as well as TMJ disorders and associated head, neck, and facial pain. Services include diagnostic sleep studies, personalized therapy plans, oral appliance fitting, and ongoing management.

The practice's main focus is obstructive sleep apnea, treated with a custom oral appliance rather than surgery. See Sleep Apnea for how it is diagnosed and CPAP Alternative if a mask has not worked for you.

Obstructive sleep apnea

The most common form, explained.

When you sleep, your breathing slows down and the soft tissues in your throat relax. For someone with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), these tissues block the upper airway repeatedly enough to disrupt normal sleep-related breathing. In some instances the tongue falls back far enough to block airflow. Oxygen levels in the blood begin to drop, causing you to wake up just long enough to regain normal breathing. These awakenings are often only a few seconds, which is why the affected person is often not aware they are repeatedly waking during the night.

Nighttime signs

  • Loud, persistent snoring
  • Insomnia, or inability to sleep
  • Witnessed pauses in breathing
  • Choking or gasping for air during sleep
  • Restless sleep
  • Frequent visits to the bathroom
  • Mouth breathing, or difficult nose breathing

Daytime signs

  • Excessive daytime fatigue
  • Early morning headaches
  • Poor concentration
  • Depression, irritability, or mood swings
  • Weight gain, high blood pressure, or diabetes
  • Anxiety, ADD, or ADHD symptoms
  • Poor job performance
  • Impotence or decreased sex drive
  • Short-term memory problems

Not everyone who snores has OSA, and not everyone with OSA snores. Koala lists obesity, a narrowed upper airway (enlarged tonsils or tongue, or a small jaw), family history, age, smoking and alcohol, high blood pressure and diabetes, and chronic nasal congestion among the causes and risk factors.

Once a diagnosis of OSA is established through a sleep study, Koala offers non-surgical treatment with oral appliance therapy, alongside changes in behavior and lifestyle such as weight loss, exercise, and avoiding smoking, drinking, and sedative medication.

Orofacial pain

Pain that starts in the mouth and spreads.

Orofacial pain is a complex issue and can be debilitating. The pain starts from the oral cavity and radiates to the head and neck region. It can have more than one origin: sometimes an acute trauma such as a heavy blow or whiplash, other times a slow process like excessive gum chewing or clenching the jaw. Temporomandibular joint disorder is the most common cause associated with it.

Common symptoms Koala describes

  • Headaches and migraines in the forehead and temple, or shooting pain up the back of the head
  • Dull, persistent pain behind the eyes, with sensitivity to light
  • Limited mouth opening, a jaw that locks, or a jaw that shifts to one side
  • Pain while chewing, or a clicking or popping jaw
  • Neck stiffness with achy, sore shoulders
  • Prolonged earaches with no infection, ringing, or dizziness

Koala's neuromuscular-trained dentists treat orofacial pain and TMJ disorder with custom-made mouthpieces called neuromuscular orthotics, along with drug-free pain relief. See TMJ Disorder for the treatment steps.

Sleep apnea & diabetes

Two conditions that feed each other.

Obstructive sleep apnea is very common in people with diabetes, and vice versa. Koala's page cites a 2013 study in Family Medicine finding that nearly half of adults with type 2 diabetes are at high risk for OSA, with many cases undiagnosed.

OSA can increase blood glucose levels because of the stress associated with long-term sleep deprivation and abrupt awakenings during the night. Over time, these increases can contribute to insulin resistance. Diabetes can also contribute to OSA: extra weight around the organs and neck causes the tissues in the neck and throat to fall into the airway.

Koala notes that the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends anyone with type 2 diabetes be evaluated for obstructive sleep apnea by a board-certified sleep medicine physician. A sleep study determines whether you have OSA and how severe it is. Koala's providers treat OSA with a comfortable oral appliance, similar to a mouthguard.

Weight loss

Is an untreated sleep disorder in the way?

If you are struggling with unwanted weight gain or the inability to lose weight, it could be due to an untreated sleep disorder. Someone with no energy will have a hard time losing weight, and if the lack of energy comes from untreated sleep apnea, there may be more working against them than fatigue.

Koala explains the hormone side: when you do not get enough sleep, leptin levels drop, so you do not feel as satisfied after you eat, and ghrelin levels rise, so your appetite is stimulated. The two acting together can set the stage for unwanted weight gain.

Signs you may have sleep apnea

  • Loud, heavy snoring
  • Periods of not breathing during sleep
  • High blood pressure
  • Easily frustrated, irritable, or depressed
  • Drowsy driving
  • Morning headaches
  • Heart disease
  • Weight gain or inability to lose weight
  • Diabetes
  • Acid reflux
  • Not feeling rested after a full night's sleep

Migraines

When the jaw is the trigger.

A migraine is a neurological disease with symptoms that can last for an hour, a day, or several days: a throbbing or pulsing sensation, nausea, pain in the ears, vision disruption known as auras, neck and shoulder tension, and extreme sensitivity to light and sound. Migraines and headaches are not the same thing.

Koala lists five common triggers: environment, stress, physical factors, food and drink, and sleep patterns including sleep apnea. Radiating pain in the face, jaw, ears, or neck can indicate TMJ disorder, and the physical stress from an untreated TMJ disorder can be a migraine trigger.

Koala's approach is to determine whether migraines are linked to issues like sleep apnea or bruxism, then build a treatment plan that may include a custom oral appliance. Normalizing the bite with a custom-fitted orthotic, in Koala's words, can help many headache sufferers. See TMJ Disorder.
