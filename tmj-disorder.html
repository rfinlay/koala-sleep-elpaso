Services · TMJ Disorder

A jaw that clicks, locks, or aches.

Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorder affects the jaw joint, causing pain, discomfort, and difficulty moving the jaw. It can result from injury, stress, or misalignment, and it affects daily functions like chewing and speaking.

What it is

The joint just above your ear.

TMD is a condition involving the temporomandibular joint, bone and cartilage resembling a ball-and-socket that sits right above your ear canal on either side of your head. When the joint slips out of position, pain can result. TMD is characterized by clicking or popping of the jaw when you open or close your mouth, persistent headaches, and sometimes chronic pain.

Koala notes that many patients arrive complaining of headaches, jaw and facial pain, and pain in the ears, never realizing the cause was a misalignment in the jaw. Some people report no pain but still have problems using their jaws. Koala's page cites approximately 35 million people in the United States with TMJ problems, and says that while both men and women experience TMJ disorders, the majority of those seeking treatment are women in their 20s and 30s.

The cause of TMD is not clear, but dentists believe symptoms arise from problems with the muscles of the jaw or with parts of the joint itself. A heavy blow or whiplash is a common cause. Other possible causes Koala lists:

  • Grinding or clenching the teeth, which puts a lot of pressure on the joint
  • Tooth loss or misalignment of teeth
  • Dislocation of the soft cushion or disc between the ball and socket
  • Osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis in the joint
  • Stress, which can tighten facial and jaw muscles or cause clenching
  • Excessive gum chewing or nail biting

Diagnosis and treatment

Two steps, starting with the true rest position.

Diagnosis and treatment for TMD at Koala may use technologies such as cone beam volumetric tomography, K-7 electromyography, and TENS (transcutaneous electrical neural stimulation). With TENS, electrodes are applied to the jaw, neck, and shoulder areas. A painless electrical current increases blood flow to the joint, relieves stiffness, improves mobility, and acts as a drug-free pain reliever.

  1. Find the jaw's true rest position

    Using TENS, the Koala® doctor is able to relax muscle memory and find the optimum true rest position for the jaw. A custom-made appliance called a neuromuscular orthotic is created to promote healing and maintain the new, corrected bite position.

  2. Permanent stabilization, if needed

    Whether this is necessary is determined after step one. Options may include wearing a long-term orthotic, orthodontic treatment, crowns or veneers on the affected teeth, or a neuromuscular denture.
