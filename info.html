Findings report · Website template, all locations

Every Koala location page tells Google the clinic is in Dunlap, Illinois.

This is not an El Paso problem. It is one template, replicated at all four locations, carrying one hard-coded business record. The same defects appear on Peoria, Bloomington, Wausau and El Paso. Every finding below was verified directly against the live pages and can be reproduced in a browser in under five minutes.

The business problem

One template. One hard-coded business record. Four locations wearing it.

The four location pages are a single template with the headline text swapped. Everything underneath is shared, including the part that should be different at every location: the business identity itself.

Each page carries a block of machine-readable code that tells search engines who and where this business is. On all four pages that block is identical. It names one street address, one ZIP code, one set of GPS coordinates and one phone number, all belonging to the Illinois clinic.

So the El Paso page publishes El Paso in its visible text and Dunlap, Illinois in the code Google actually reads. Bloomington does the same. So does Wausau. Three of the four locations are, in machine-readable form, claiming to be somewhere they are not.

This is why it matters commercially. Marketing spend on any one location routes patients to a page that quietly contradicts that location's own Google Business Profile. The contradiction is invisible on the page, invisible to automated testing, and repeated everywhere. It is the kind of defect that does not announce itself, it just quietly costs.

Why it is worth fixing once

Because it is one template, this is not four repairs. It is one correction that propagates to every location, and to every location added later. The same property that caused the problem is what makes it cheap to solve.

Stated plainly, up front

There is already a platform vendor in the seat. This is not a pitch to replace them.

The site runs on WordPress under a theme named patientgain-2021-april. That is the signature of PatientGain, a healthcare marketing platform that sells HIPAA-compliant sites on a recurring per-location contract.

We are naming them because pretending otherwise would waste your time. There is an incumbent, that relationship presumably exists for good reasons including compliance, and nothing in this document asks anyone to unwind it.

These findings are handed over as a defect report. If PatientGain fixes them, the problem is solved and that is a good outcome. Every item below is specific enough to forward to them and act on directly. If it turns out to be useful to have someone outside the platform look at the template, that conversation can happen later.

Scope is deliberately narrow. Public marketing pages only: structured data, images, page speed, navigation and metadata. Nothing touching patient intake, patient communication, or any system carrying protected health information. That boundary is not negotiable and it exists so that the compliance question never has to be reopened.

4 of 4 pages

  • The El Paso page's main photograph is the Bloomington clinic.

    The desktop banner on every location page is the same file, bloomington-header-img-desktop.jpg. On the El Paso page its alt text reads "Welcome to Koala Center For Sleep & TMJ Disorders in El Paso, TX." The label says El Paso. The photograph is Bloomington.

    The mobile version is worse, because it looks localized and is not. The file named el-paso-mobile-header.jpg is byte-for-byte identical to the Bloomington file. Same size, same checksum. A copy was renamed and shipped.

    This also governs link previews. The social share image on all four pages is that same Bloomington photograph. Every time anyone shares any Koala location link, in a text message or on social media, the thumbnail is Bloomington.

  • "Call Clinic" does not call the clinic.

    The link appears three times per page. On every location it redirects to one generic corporate contact page, where the only phone number present is the Illinois number, and it is not tappable.

  • Mobile load time is 6.2 seconds. On all four.

    Google's threshold for a good experience is 2.5 seconds; above 4.0 is rated poor. Every location page measures exactly 6.2 seconds on mobile, and the fact that all four land on the same figure is itself proof of a shared template.

  • A map downloads on every page and never appears.

    The Google Maps library is the single largest asset on the page at 378 KB. It loads, throws an error because the element it needs is missing, and no map is ever shown. That weight is paid on every visit for nothing.

  • The same menu is stamped in three times.

    Within one page, the sleep apnea link appears eight times, TMJ eight times, snoring eight times, appointments three times. The same inventory repeats in the header, an inline block and the footer.

  • Automated testing scores this a perfect 100 for SEO.

    Every location page scores 100 out of 100 on the standard SEO audit, while telling Google it is in the wrong state. Automated tools check whether the code is valid, not whether it is true. Desktop performance scores 98 to 100 as well, so a quick test on a laptop shows almost nothing wrong.

    If anyone checks this, ask them to test on a phone. That is where the 6.2 seconds shows up, and it is where the patients are.

What we would do

Corrections to the template, and what each one buys.

Ordered by value against effort. Every one is a template-level change, which means it lands at all four locations at once.

  • Give each location its own business record. Real address, real coordinates, real local phone in the code search engines read. Outcome: your own website stops contradicting your Google Business Profile. This is the cheapest item on the list and there is no argument for leaving it.
  • Make every phone number tappable. A real local number at the top of each page, tappable, plus in the header and footer. Outcome: the patient who is ready to call can call, instead of arriving at a contact page for another state.
  • Localize the photography and the share image. Each location shows its own clinic and its own team. Outcome: a shared link previews the right place, and a prospective patient recognizes the office they are about to walk into.
  • Remove the map library that never renders. 378 KB and a JavaScript error removed from every page load, with either a working map or a static one in its place. Outcome: the single largest speed win available, and it costs nothing in features because the map does not currently appear.
  • Bring mobile load under the 2.5 second threshold. Modern image formats, deferred offscreen images, connection hints, unused code removed. Outcome: the page holds the visitor who arrived from a search on their phone at eleven at night, which is when people research sleep problems.
  • Give the page a hierarchy. One clear path from problem, to treatment, to the doctor, to booking, instead of the same menu three times. Outcome: a visitor can find the answer to their question without hunting for it.
  • Lead with the doctor's credentials. On the El Paso page, military service and specialist training currently sit buried mid-paragraph. Outcome: in a city built around a military installation, that is not a footnote, it is the reason a patient chooses one clinic over another.
  • Fix the small breakages. A misspelled internal link, two JavaScript errors on every page load, and hours that disagree between the location page and the FAQ page. Outcome: nothing dramatic, just the absence of small signals that nobody is watching.

What the corrections are for

Outcomes, stated without exaggeration.

What follows is what these changes are designed to achieve. Where a claim cannot be proven, it is not made.

Your website stops arguing with your listings

Right now each page publishes a location that disagrees with its own Business Profile. Correcting it removes a contradiction that costs nothing to fix and has no upside in keeping.

The ready-to-call patient can call

The moment a patient decides to act is the moment the current page offers them an out-of-state number they cannot tap. That is the least expensive loss on this list to eliminate.

Shared links show the right clinic

Every referral, text message and social share currently previews the same Illinois photograph regardless of which location was shared.

Mobile visitors stay long enough to read

6.2 seconds is well past the point where people leave. Most sleep-apnea research happens on a phone, late, by someone already tired.

One fix, every location

Because these are template defects, correcting them once applies everywhere, including any location opened later. The property that caused the problem is what makes it cheap.

The brand looks maintained

Consistency is the argument for this work, not against it. This is not a different page for one location. It is a corrected template for all of them.

Calibration

What this document does not claim.

A findings report is only useful if its limits are stated as clearly as its findings.

Not a ranking promise

No rankings, Business Profiles or directory listings were measured. Proximity to the searcher dominates local search and no website change affects it. The defensible claim is narrower: the site contradicts itself, and that is free to fix.

Not an accessibility crisis

Accessibility already scores 99 out of 100. There is no compliance emergency here, and any work should be held to leaving that score untouched or better.

Not a broken site

Mobile performance scores in the low seventies. That is mediocre, not broken. The unambiguous failure is the 6.2 second load and the wrong location data, not the site as a whole.

Not a criticism of any location

Nothing here reflects a decision made locally. These are template-level defects that no individual location controls or could have prevented.

Not a compliance proposal

Nothing proposed touches intake, patient communication or protected health information. Public marketing pages only, deliberately.

Not an assumption about the contract

No knowledge is claimed of any agreement, term, renewal date or scope with the existing vendor. These findings are offered to whoever is best placed to act on them.

Path forward

Five steps, and the first three cost nothing.

  1. Verify one finding yourself

    Open any location page on a phone and try to tap the phone number. Then share the link and look at the preview image. Both take under a minute and neither requires taking anyone's word for it.

  2. Confirm the correct local phone number for each location

    More than one number appears in public sources for at least one location. This should be settled internally before anything is corrected, because it is the one input that has to come from you.

  3. Forward the findings to whoever maintains the site

    Every item here is specific enough to act on. The structured-data correction and the photography swap are likely a single day of work for whoever already has access.

  4. Decide whether the template gets corrected or refreshedYour call

    Correcting the existing template is the smaller path. The concept linked below shows what a rebuilt location page looks like, as a reference point for that decision rather than a proposal.

  5. Roll the correction across all locations at onceYour call

    Whatever is decided should land network-wide in one change, with the mobile load time and the accessibility score used as the before-and-after acceptance test.
